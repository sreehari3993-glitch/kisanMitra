# Visual Diagnostic Guide: Soil Nutrient Deficiencies & Pest Remedies

This field diagnostic manual provides farmers and agronomists with rapid visual symptom identification guides, root causes, and corrective bio-chemical and agronomic remedies.

---

## 1. Macronutrient Deficiency Symptoms & Remediation

### Nitrogen ($N$) Deficiency
- **Visual Symptoms**: General chlorosis (uniform yellowing) appearing first on the oldest lower leaves, while upper young leaves remain pale light green. Stunted, slender stems; premature leaf dropping; severely reduced tillering in cereals.
- **Biochemical Cause**: Nitrogen is highly mobile within the plant. When deficient, the plant translocates nitrogen from older tissues to young growing tips.
- **Corrective Action**:
  - Immediate Relief: Foliar spray of 1%–2% aqueous Urea solution during cool evening hours.
  - Soil Treatment: Topdress with 25–40 kg/acre Urea under adequate soil moisture conditions.

### Phosphorus ($P$) Deficiency
- **Visual Symptoms**: Dark green foliage turning purplish-bronze or red along leaf margins and veins, starting on older leaves. Severely restricted root branching; delayed flowering, poor fruit set, and sluggish grain maturity.
- **Biochemical Cause**: Impaired nucleotide synthesis and energy transfer (ATP/ADP), resulting in anthocyanin pigment accumulation. Often induced by acidic soils ($\text{pH} < 5.5$) or highly alkaline soils ($\text{pH} > 8.0$) fixing phosphate ions.
- **Corrective Action**:
  - Soil Treatment: Basal application of Diammonium Phosphate (DAP) or Single Superphosphate (SSP) placed 5–7 cm below seed depth.
  - Soil Amendment: Broadcast Agricultural Lime in acidic soils to raise pH above 6.0 and unbind phosphorus.

### Potassium ($K$) Deficiency
- **Visual Symptoms**: Marginal leaf necrosis and chlorosis ("tip burn" or "leaf scorch") starting at the tips of older leaves and advancing inward along the borders. Stems become weak and brittle, leading to crop lodging. Grains and fruits remain small, shriveled, and tasteless.
- **Biochemical Cause**: Reduced osmotic regulation and stomatal control, causing localized dehydration and toxic ammonium accumulation at leaf margins.
- **Corrective Action**:
  - Soil Treatment: Apply Muriate of Potash (MOP, 60% K₂O) or Potassium Sulfate at 20–35 kg/acre.
  - Foliar Spray: Spray 1% Potassium Nitrate ($\text{KNO}_3$) at flowering and fruit-set stages.

## 2. Secondary & Micronutrient Deficiency Symptoms

### Zinc ($Zn$) Deficiency
- **Visual Symptoms**: Broad white or bleached bands on both sides of the midrib on young leaves ("white bud" in maize, "Khaira" disease in rice). Rosetting and leaf size reduction ("little leaf" symptom).
- **Corrective Action**: Apply Zinc Sulfate ($\text{ZnSO}_4 \cdot 7\text{H}_2\text{O}$) at 10–15 kg/acre into soil, or foliar spray 0.5% $\text{ZnSO}_4$ neutralized with 0.25% slaked lime.

### Iron ($Fe$) Deficiency
- **Visual Symptoms**: Striking interveinal chlorosis on the youngest upper leaves: veins remain dark green while the intervening leaf lamina turns pale yellow or ivory white. Common in calcareous alkaline soils ($\text{pH} > 7.5$).
- **Corrective Action**: Foliar spray of 0.5% Ferrous Sulfate ($\text{FeSO}_4$) + 0.1% citric acid, or application of chelated iron (Fe-EDTA).

### Boron ($B$) Deficiency
- **Visual Symptoms**: Death of apical growing points; distorted, brittle leaves; internal browning or cracking in fruits (papaya, pomegranate); hollow stem in cabbage/cauliflower.
- **Corrective Action**: Soil application of Borax (5–10 kg/acre) or foliar spray of Solubor (0.15%–0.2%) prior to flowering.

## 3. Major Crop Pests & Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

### Yellow Stem Borer (*Scirpophaga incertulas*) in Rice
- **Symptoms**: Central shoot drying in vegetative stage ("dead heart") or empty, erect, bleached panicles in reproductive stage ("white earhead").
- **IPM Remedy**:
  - Set up pheromone traps (5 per acre) for monitoring.
  - Release egg parasitoid *Trichogramma japonicum* @ 40,000/acre at weekly intervals.
  - Apply neem oil (1500 ppm) @ 3 ml/liter of water at early moth emergence.

### Sucking Pests (Aphids, Whiteflies, Thrips) in Cotton & Vegetables
- **Symptoms**: Downward or upward cupping of leaves; sticky honeydew on foliage followed by black sooty mold; premature leaf drop and virus transmission (e.g. leaf curl virus).
- **IPM Remedy**:
  - Install yellow and blue sticky traps (10–12 traps/acre) above crop canopy level.
  - Spray Neem Seed Kernel Extract (NSKE 5%) or *Verticillium lecanii* bio-fungicide @ 5 g/liter.

### Fall Armyworm (*Spodoptera frugiperda*) in Maize
- **Symptoms**: Pin-hole feeding on young leaves, large irregular holes and abundant moist sawdust-like fecal frass inside the central leaf whorl.
- **IPM Remedy**:
  - Erection of bird perches (10/acre) to encourage predatory birds.
  - Apply sand or sawdust mixed with lime (9:1 ratio) directly into leaf whorls.
  - Spray *Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt) @ 2 g/liter or biopesticide *Metarhizium rileyi*.
